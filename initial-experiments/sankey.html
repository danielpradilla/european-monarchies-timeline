A Sankey of 1519 needs a number the data doesn't have

Four ruling houses on the left, six crowns on the right, joined by seven reigns that were running in 1519. The houses, crowns and reigns are real records from the timeline's dataset. The widths are mine: the dataset has no quantity for these links, so a Sankey has nothing to measure.

The widths are invented layout units. They do not measure territory, population, revenue, armies or power. The two buttons swap one made-up set of weights for another. The seven links stay the same records, and the picture changes anyway.

What the chart gets wrong

This is a selection of crowns and rulers alive in 1519, not all of them. Each link is a reign record that assigns a person and a crown to a house. Nothing moves from left to right, and the links say nothing about territory changing hands, descent or two states merging.

The records also repeat people. Charles V draws two links and Joanna draws three, so the Sankey counts each of them more than once. Spain takes a link from each house because the dataset keeps a Spain lane for the dynastic monarchy of 1479 to 1716 next to Castile and Aragon, which kept separate institutions. Joanna held the royal titles until 1555, while Charles exercised government and, from 1516, shared the titles with her. A Sankey adds the two Spain links together, as if the crown were twice as big.

With equal weights the chart is a count of records. Triple the Habsburg links and the Habsburgs fill more than half the drawing. No record changed between the two views.
