# Historical publication audit

Initial editorial review recorded 13 July 2026; this is not certification of every claim. The reviewed publication surface is the canonical dataset in `data/timeline.json`, the interactive explorer, methodology and essay.

## Standard used

- Separate a crown, a dynasty, a composite monarchy and a state; sharing a ruler is not treated as state merger.
- Treat years such as 395, 843, 872, 925, 962, 1139 and 1299 as periodization choices when the evidence does not support a clean foundation date.
- Distinguish asserted titles, effective rule and recognition by other powers.
- Preserve interruptions, rival reigns and republics where the annual-scale model can show them; state sub-year interruptions in notes.
- Prefer official archives and institutions for constitutional acts and current sovereigns, and scholarly or national reference works for disputed early and medieval claims. Wikipedia remains only as broad navigation for many uncontroversial records.

## Corrections made

- Reframed 395 as an enduring division between Roman imperial courts, not the legal creation of two unrelated empires.
- Split the eastern Roman lane at 1204 and 1261 to show the crusader conquest, Nicaean successor government and restoration of Constantinople.
- Removed the false Leonid house assignment from Majorian and limited the western Leonid segment to Anthemius.
- Split elective imperial dynasty bands that had wrongly shown continuous Hohenstaufen, Luxembourg and Habsburg possession.
- Restored the non-Carolingian rulers of West Francia to the dynasty layer.
- Added England's 1649–1660 Interregnum and Scotland's Cromwellian interruption.
- Removed Stephen of Blois from the Norman house band and Macbeth and Lulach from the Dunkeld band.
- Labelled 843 for Scotland and 872 for Norway as traditional chronologies rather than secure state foundations.
- Corrected early Aragon and Navarre dynasty assignments.
- Replaced a single 1715 Spanish transition with the documented 1707–1716 Nueva Planta sequence; added the Bonaparte monarchy and the First Republic interruption.
- Split Philip V's two reigns around Louis I's reign in 1724.
- Reworked Poland's Piast and Wettin bands and Hungary's fifteenth-century dynasties so rival and non-dynastic reigns are not hidden.
- Recast Sicily and Naples as separate crowns after 1282 and removed dynasty bands that falsely treated them as one state.
- Added the French annexation gap in Monaco and the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic interruptions to the Papal States.
- Qualified Tomislav's title, the 1102 Croatian–Hungarian settlement and the later *Pacta conventa* text.
- Changed Denmark–Norway from a simple personal union to an unequal composite monarchy.
- Corrected title changes for Philip II of France, Louis XVI, Frederick II of Prussia, Simeon I, Alexander I of Yugoslavia, Honoré II and Pius IX.
- Replaced the claim that four imperial monarchies fell in 1918 with the accurate 1917–1922 sequence.
- Verified the 2024 Danish and 2025 Luxembourg, Andorran and Vatican transitions against official institutions.

## Publication limits

The timeline is selective, not a complete ruler list. Its annual scale cannot visually separate events only months apart, such as the Hundred Days, the Spanish transitions of 1873–1874 or the Roman Republic of 1849; notes identify those cases. Medieval territorial boundaries are not encoded. Source links support public navigation and the principal editorial decisions, but the project is not a substitute for a critical prosopography or a full scholarly bibliography for every reign.

Regression tests in `tests/data.test.js` preserve the most consequential corrections, while `scripts/validate_data.js` checks chronology, open-ended current records, references and entity relationships.
